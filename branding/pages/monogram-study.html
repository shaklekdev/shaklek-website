Shaklek · monogram study

The letter you already own

A short mark for the places the full lockup cannot go — a woven tab, a button, a browser tab, a sticker, a stitched cuff. It does not replace the wordmark. Nobody knows this brand yet, so the wordmark still has to do the naming; the monogram is what people recognise afterwards.

The connection worth noticing: the Arabic letter ش is "sh". It is the first letter of شكلك and the sound your campaign is built on. One mark, doing three jobs at once — Arabic initial, Latin initials, and the gesture.

Five candidates

Each set in the faces you already use: Italiana for Latin, Reem Kufi for Arabic. Nothing new has been introduced.

S
too anonymous S alone Elegant, and it belongs to everyone. A single Italiana S is a hundred other labels. It also throws away the "sh".
SH

ش
my pick ش, the Arabic sheen Distinctive, unmistakably yours, even-stroked — the only one of the five that can be embroidered. And it is "sh".

The "shhhhh" idea

It is a good hook, and it is better than a pun. Say it out loud and it is already the brand's tone of voice: quiet, unhurried, the opposite of a sale.

Shhhhhh

The gesture works because it is a secret, not a joke — the piece nobody else has, the one that was cut for you. That is a truthful thing for a made-to-order label to say, which is what stops it being a gag that wears out in a month.

  • What it can carry: "Shhh. It's made for you." · "Nobody else has this one." · A finger to the lips, and the ش appearing.
  • What it should not become: a discount voice. "Shhh, secret sale" burns the idea on the cheapest possible use and cannot be taken back.
  • Where it belongs: Reels and stories, where sound is the point. It is a spoken idea — it does almost nothing on a still image or a static ad.
  • The reason it fits Shaklek specifically: ش is the sound and the letter. The mark on the garment and the noise in the video are the same thing. Most brands have to invent that link; you already have it.

What I would do

Adopt ش as the short mark. Keep the full lockup as the logo. The wordmark names the brand while nobody knows it; the ش is what goes on the seal, the tab, the cuff and the favicon — and it is the only candidate here that can actually be stitched.

Do not build the campaign yet. It needs a real reel, a real voice and a real garment reveal, and it is worth more once there is a customer to film it for. Write it down, keep the mark, come back to it when there is something to be quiet about.
