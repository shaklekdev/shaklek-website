# Kinda chic, the campaign, and the trap in it

Written overnight 2026-08-25 on the founder's brief. Read the first section
before using any line below. The brief as given would have produced a post that
gets ratioed, and the reason is worth two minutes.

---

## 1. What the trend actually is

Researched before writing, not assumed.

The "kinda chic" format began April 2026 and went wide over July and August. It
is a **body-positivity and wellbeing** format: you describe something
deliberately **un**glamorous as chic, explicitly *instead of* luxury. Soft
yellow caption, carousel or short video, tone between earnest and wry.

Real examples in the wild:

- "Kinda chic to be in your 50s" (Drew Barrymore)
- "Kinda chic to repeat outfits"
- "Kinda chic to cheer on other women"

Notice what none of them are: a product. The format's whole charge comes from
refusing the flex. That is why it spread.

### The trap

The brief's example lines are product features wearing the trend's clothes:

- ~~"Kinda chic to customize your own shirt"~~
- ~~"Kinda chic to get a tailor cut your favourite trousers"~~
- ~~"Kinda chic to wear breathable and fashionable pieces"~~

Each one takes a format about accepting your body and turns it into an ad. On a
trend built on refusing luxury signalling, a brand posting its own feature list
reads as exactly the thing the trend is against. This is the single most
likely way to spend the brand's first real social moment badly.

### The fit that does work

Shaklek's actual proposition already lives in this space:

> Clothes cut to the body you have, instead of you being sorted into someone's
> size chart.

That is a body-positivity statement that happens to be true of the product. It
needs no hijack. Every line in section 3 is written from there.

**"Kinda chic to not have to go to the tailor yourself" is the one brief line
that survives**, it is about the person's time and effort, not the feature
list. It is kept, reworded, in the B set.

## 2. Rules every line here obeys

From the founder's corrections, carried by Session D, plus this project's own
claim discipline:

1. **No em dashes.** They read as generated text. Commas, full stops, or a line
   break instead.
2. **No AI mentions, ever, customer facing.**
3. **Never call the imagery a photograph**, and never "the actual piece you will
   receive". The catalog images are generated. Say what the customer *does*, not
   what the medium is.
4. **"Timeless" and "essentials" are load-bearing.** The niche is timeless
   fashion essentials: elegant, customizable, skin friendly, cotton or linen.
5. **Every claim must be true of what ships today.** No unlimited customization
   (pockets and closure are fixed at their defaults until Shaklek+), no camera
   measurement, no promises the tailor cannot keep.
6. **Meta rejects creative implying knowledge of the viewer's body.** Second
   person body references are organic only. Each line below is marked.

## 3. The lines

Ranked within each set. `ORGANIC` means do not put it in a paid ad, per rule 6.
`SAFE` means either.

### A. The body ones. Strongest, most on format.

| Line | | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Kinda chic to have never been a medium | ORGANIC | The truest line the brand owns. Names something people feel and never say. Sits perfectly in the trend because it is a confession, not a boast. |
| Kinda chic to stop blaming your body for the fit | ORGANIC | The emotional centre of made to order. The garment was wrong, not the person. |
| Kinda chic to be between two sizes forever | ORGANIC | Universal. Almost everyone is. |
| Kinda chic to know your own measurements | SAFE | Quietly flattering, and it is the one thing a Shaklek customer actually does. |
| Kinda chic to buy one thing that fits | SAFE | Anti haul. Sits against fast fashion without naming it. |
| Kinda chic to stop apologising for your shoulders | ORGANIC | Specific beats general. Shoulders are the most common ready to wear failure. |
| Kinda chic to want sleeves that end where your arms do | SAFE | Wry, physical, and it names the real defect in ready to wear. |

### B. The effort and time ones. On format, gently practical.

| Line | | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Kinda chic to never sit in a tailor's queue again | SAFE | The surviving brief line, reworded to be about the person's afternoon rather than the service. |
| Kinda chic to skip the alterations appointment | SAFE | Most people already pay a tailor twice. This names it without a price claim. |
| Kinda chic to let the clothes do the adjusting | SAFE | Quiet, and it is literally what made to order means. |
| Kinda chic to only own things you actually wear | SAFE | Timeless essentials, said as a value rather than a product line. |

### C. The place and fabric ones. Local, ownable, true.

| Line | | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Kinda chic to dress for August in Dubai | SAFE | A Zara ad cannot say this. Linen makes it true. |
| Kinda chic to choose fabric you can breathe in | SAFE | True: cotton or linen only. Avoids the word "breathable" as a spec claim and makes it a choice the customer makes. |
| Kinda chic to wear linen in a lift | SAFE | Funny, local, specific. Strong TikTok opener. |
| Kinda chic to own four things and love them | SAFE | Carries "essentials" without saying it. |

### D. Rejected, and why. Keep this list.

| Line | Why not |
|---|---|
| Kinda chic to customize your own shirt | Product feature in a body-positivity format. The trap in section 1. |
| Kinda chic to get a tailor to cut your favourite trousers | Same, and it implies we alter a garment the customer already owns. We do not. We make a new one. |
| Kinda chic to wear breathable and fashionable pieces | Reads as a spec sheet. "Fashionable" is a claim about taste that the viewer decides, not the brand. |
| Kinda chic to design your own clothes | Overclaims. The customizer offers fixed options, not design. |
| Kinda chic to get it in 10 days | An offer, not a confession. Belongs in retargeting creative, not here. |

## 4. The visual

The trend has a signature and it should be honoured, not redesigned:

- **Soft yellow caption** over the image. This is the recognisable element. A
  Shaklek-neutral cream or navy caption would read as a different post and lose
  the borrowed reach.
- Lowercase, generous letter spacing, one line where possible.
- Caption sits low, over quiet space in the image, never over the garment's
  hem or the model's face.
- Carousel of three to five, or a short video with the caption held still.

Rendered files land in `brand-assets/INSTA/kinda-chic/`, which is gitignored.
The assets are on disk only.
