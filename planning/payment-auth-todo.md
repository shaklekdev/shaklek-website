- [x] **Clerk**, chosen over Cognito and over Auth.js/NextAuth. Reasoning: Cognito is the "stays inside AWS" option but has real, well-known developer-experience friction for custom flows — not worth it for a solo maintainer. Auth.js is free and flexible but self-hosted: you own the session logic, the database adapter tables, and keeping it patched. Clerk is a fully managed service — prebuilt sign-in UI, session handling, and magic-link/social login all done for you — which means the least ongoing maintenance of the three, and its free tier (50,000 monthly retained users, corrected 2026-08-16) comfortably covers pilot scale. "Easiest" and "cheapest" point the same direction here, which is why it won over Auth.js despite Auth.js being nominally free.

### Shaklek+ feature shortlist — researched 2026-08-17, not built
Recommended launch bundle, cheapest/lowest-risk first:
- **Digital wardrobe / style history** — persist past orders and saved designs as a browsable "wardrobe" on `/account`. Mostly a display layer on data that already exists (`orders`, `order_items`), no new AI/vendor cost.
- **Priority tailoring turnaround** — subscriber orders queue-jump on `/dashboard/orders`. Pure ops/process change, zero engineering cost.
- **Extra alteration coverage** — e.g. a second free alteration, or a longer window than the standard 14 days. A pricing/policy decision, not a build.
- **Camera-based precise sizing** — see below. Gated behind real subscriber revenue, not launch-ready.
- **AI styling assistant** (recommend color/fit from saved profile) — moderate build cost (Gemini calls per use, cheap per-call vs. a flat vendor subscription), reasonable second-wave addition once the core loop is proven.
- **Virtual try-on / avatar** — deprioritized. Higher build complexity (photorealistic garment-on-person generation is expensive and quality is a real risk), and it's the kind of feature people try once rather than return to monthly, so it's a weak fit for retention specifically.
- **Upload-your-own with an on-demand color palette** (requested 2026-08-17) — two separable pieces:
  - *Upload + human-made-to-spec*: already mostly built. `/upload` and the shared `CustomizeChat` flow exist in the codebase (currently unlinked from the live nav, see `web-design-todo.md`), routing a freeform request + reference photo to a human stylist. Re-surfacing this behind a Shaklek+ gate is cheap: mostly a subscription check + re-linking it, not new engineering.
  - *Live AI preview in any color the customer picks*: this is a different, much bigger thing. Confirmed via grep, **zero live AI image generation exists anywhere in the codebase today** — every catalog photo is a static pre-rendered PNG (`public/catalog/`), and even the freeform upload flow's "preview" is just the customer's own uploaded photo, not an AI rendering. Building this means a real-time (or near-real-time) Gemini image-edit API route, triggered per customer request, with a live per-call cost (~$0.04-0.15/generation on Flash) that scales with usage instead of being a fixed line item, plus loading/error states and rate limiting to cap abuse cost.
- **Virtual try-on, reconsidered** (requested 2026-08-17) — technically the *same* underlying build as the on-demand palette preview above (Gemini image-editing), just applied to a photo of the customer instead of a garment swatch. Worth treating as one investment, not two separate features, if both get greenlit. Extra considerations specific to try-on: the input photo is of the customer's own body (same privacy/consent weight as camera-sizing), and the quality bar is higher since a bad render of someone's own photo is a worse experience than a bad render of a mannequin, likely pushing this toward the pricier Nano Banana Pro tier (~$0.13/image at 1-2K) rather than Flash.
- **Both of the above are, by the founder's own dossier, explicitly Phase 2/3 work** (`ai-integration-todo.md`): "zero real AI exists in the product today" and "real-time generative visualization... Phase 3, gated on Phase 2 proving out with real usage data" was a deliberate decision to validate the human-run Concierge MVP first before investing in this exact engineering. Flagging that this reopens that sequencing call, not dismissing the features themselves.
- **Priority tailoring, reconsidered as "express" tier** (requested 2026-08-17) — same recommendation as above, still zero build cost, just an ops queue-priority rule.

**Camera-based sizing — vendor research, not adopted yet:**
- **3DLOOK** (Mobile Tailor / FitXpress) — 2 photos (front + side) → 80+ measurements. Pricing is a flat monthly subscription by scan volume, not pay-per-use: Mobile Tailor Basic $499/mo for up to 100 scans, Premium $999/mo for up to 500; FitXpress starts at $1,000/mo for 500 scans. 7-day trial available, no free tier. [Pricing](https://3dlook.ai/pricing/)
- **Bodygram** — API-based, pricing model looked more usage-based (per-measurement or per-user/month) rather than a flat volume subscription, but I couldn't get a reliable USD figure to quote confidently. Worth a direct pricing conversation with them if 3DLOOK's flat minimum is the blocker. [Developer docs](https://developers.bodygram.com/scan/api/)
- **Why not now**: 3DLOOK's real minimum cost (~$499-1,000/month) is a fixed recurring commitment that only makes sense once there's actual subscriber volume to amortize it against — the site currently has zero live (non-test-mode) revenue. There's also no proof any general-purpose vision model (including Gemini) is accurate enough for this without a purpose-built, validated model like 3DLOOK's or Bodygram's — this genuinely isn't a "just prompt Gemini" feature, since a wrong measurement here means a real wasted garment made by one tailor for one person, not just a bad chatbot answer. Revisit once Shaklek+ has enough paying subscribers to cover the vendor cost, or once a cheaper per-scan option (Bodygram or similar) is confirmed.
